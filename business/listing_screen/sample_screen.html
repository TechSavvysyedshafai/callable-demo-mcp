2. What drives the call

  • Price is in range. 2.3x profit sits between the two live insurance listings found (2.9x and 0.3x). Flat profit repays the estimated price in about 2.3 years.
  • Income is probably concentrated. About $636 per 1,000 views is far above display-ad income, so most revenue is likely lead sales to a few partners. Losing one could cut revenue sharply.
  • Traffic is the weak point. The listing admits a Google update hurt the site, and 2,573 views a month leaves little buffer.

3. Conditions before bidding

  • Revenue by partner shows no single payer above about 40% of revenue (my threshold).
  • Search Console shows traffic stable for the last six months.
  • Payout statements match the stated profit.
  • The writer agrees to stay at least six months.

If two of the four fail, pass.

4. Ten questions for the seller

  1. Revenue by partner for the last 24 months, and the contracts behind each.
  2. Search Console clicks and impressions for 24 months, with the date of the Google update.
  3. Top 20 pages by traffic and by revenue.
  4. Payout statements and bank deposits that match the stated profit.
  5. What the $98 of monthly costs contain; is the writer fully paid out of it?
  6. Can partners move to the new owner, and on what terms?
  7. Why does the site age differ between the listing field and the text?
  8. Any manual penalties, paid links or link-building history.
  9. What the writer is paid and whether they agree to stay.
  10. Consent, licence and disclosure language on the lead forms.

5. What this screen is not

This is a short screen for discussion. It is not a quality-of-earnings report, no CPA signs it, and it is not legal, tax or investment advice. It uses only the public listing page, did not contact the seller or buyer, and could not verify any figure against bank statements. The price is an estimate.
