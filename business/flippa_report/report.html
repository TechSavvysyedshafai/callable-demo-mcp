  • Price looks fair. At 2.3x annual profit ($18,468) the estimated price is $42,800. Flat profit pays it back in about 2.3 years.
  • Revenue per visitor is unusually high. About $636 per 1,000 page views. Ad income pays a small fraction of that, so the income is almost certainly lead sales to a few insurance partners. That is a concentration risk.
  • Traffic is the weak point. The listing says a Google core update hurt the site. At 2,573 views a month there is little buffer.
  • The simulation is mixed. In my 20,000-run model the buyer recovers the price within 36 months in 38% of runs and within 24 months in 0%.
  • Suggested bid: $29,500 to $36,900, walk away above $42,800, and only after the checks in section 8.

4. Where the money comes from

$636 per 1,000 views is not advertising income. That points to a handful of paying partners.

Unit economics (derived)

Revenue per page view$0.64
Profit per page view$0.60

What it implies

  • Display ads typically pay a few dollars to a few tens of dollars per 1,000 views (my general assumption, not verified for this site). Here it is about $636.
  • So most income is probably lead-gen or referral fees from insurance partners.
  • Costs of $98 a month leave no room for the freelance writer to be fully paid. The buyer should expect to add spend.
  • A few large buyers of leads mean losing one could cut revenue by a large share.

8. Questions to ask before bidding

Ten checks, in the order they would change the price.

  1. Revenue by partner for the last 24 months, and the contracts behind each.
  2. Google Search Console clicks and impressions for 24 months, with the date of the update.
  3. Top 20 pages by traffic and by revenue; how many carry the income.
  4. Payout statements and bank deposits that match the stated profit.
  5. What the $98 of monthly costs contain; is the writer fully paid out of it.
  6. Whether partners can be moved to the new owner and on what terms.
  7. Why the site age differs between the field and the text.
  8. Any manual penalties, link purchases or paid-link history.
  9. What the writer is paid, how long they have worked on it, and whether they agree to stay.
  10. Any consent, licence or disclosure language on the lead forms.

Verdict: proceed with conditions. I would bid $29,500 to $36,900, which is 1.6x to 2.0x annual profit, and walk away above $42,800.

Conditions: revenue by partner shows no single payer above about 40% (my threshold); Search Console shows traffic stable for six months; payouts match the stated profit; the writer agrees to stay for six months.

If two of the four conditions fail, pass. At that point the multiple no longer compensates for the risk.
