## Lines
- @0.5 {rise} Why does squeezing a gas make more ammonia?
- @4.5 Nitrogen and hydrogen join to make ammonia. Ammonia splits back too.
- @10.0 When both rates match, the mix stops changing. That is equilibrium.
- @14.6 {whoosh} Squeeze the piston. Pressure jumps.
- @18.0 Four molecules become two, so it makes more ammonia.
- @23.8 {tick} Now heat it. Making ammonia gives out heat,
- @27.5 so the reaction runs backwards. Ammonia drops.
- @32.0 {pop} Pull ammonia out, and it makes more.
- @38.4 {tick} A catalyst only speeds it up. It never changes the end point.
- @44.0 {rise} Change anything, and equilibrium pushes back. That is Le Chatelier.

## Facts
- N2 + 3H2 <=> 2NH3, dH = -92.4 kJ/mol (exothermic). Sources: ausetute.com.au Haber process page; Wikipedia gives -92.28 kJ. Checked 26 Nov 2026.
- Kp = 1.64e-4 atm^-2 at 400 C, 1.45e-5 at 500 C (Wikipedia Haber process table; 400 C value also in a standard textbook problem).
- 4 gas molecules on the left, 2 on the right, so higher pressure favours ammonia (NCERT Class 11 Chemistry, Unit 6; standard).
- Measured equilibrium NH3 at 200 atm: 36.3% at 400 C, 17.6% at 500 C (Larson and Dodge data, quoted in Brown 1926). The page's ideal-gas model gives 35.1% at 400 C.
- A catalyst speeds forward and reverse equally and does not move the equilibrium (NCERT, standard).
- Rates in the simulation are illustrative; amounts at equilibrium come from the real Kp.
