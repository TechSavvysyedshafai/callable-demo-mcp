## Lines
- @0.5 {rise} Slice a cone. How many different curves can you get?
- @4.8 Flat cut: a circle. Tilt it, and you get an ellipse.
- @11.6 Tilt until the plane matches the side of the cone. A parabola.
- @18.2 Go steeper, and it cuts both cones. A hyperbola.
- @25.0 {pop} Now drop two balls in, touching the cone and the plane.
- @29.6 They touch the plane exactly at the two foci.
- @33.4 For every point P, the two distances add up to 2a.
- @40.4 {tick} Move the cut, and it still holds.
- @44.6 {rise} One number decides the curve: the eccentricity, e.

## Facts
- Circle, ellipse, parabola and hyperbola are the plane sections of a double cone (NCERT Class 11 Mathematics, Conic Sections; standard).
- With cone half-angle a and plane tilt b from horizontal: ellipse when b < 90 - a, parabola when b = 90 - a, hyperbola when b > 90 - a; e = sin b / cos a (derived and checked numerically in this repo, 26 Nov 2026).
- Dandelin spheres touch the cutting plane at the foci (Dandelin, 1822; standard). For an ellipse PF1 + PF2 = 2a; hyperbola |PF1 - PF2| = 2a; parabola PF = distance to directrix (NCERT definitions). The page measures these live from the drawn points.
- Cone half-angle 30 degrees, so the parabola appears at a 60 degree tilt.
