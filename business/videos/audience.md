# Audience: Fifty Second Syllabus
Data: LIVE (40 quotes from 8 YouTube videos, one source type) · Updated: 2026-11-16 · provisional until course reviews and forum threads are added

## Segments
1. **Indian Class 11-12 NEET/JEE aspirants.** Watch long "one shot" lectures from Physics Wallah and similar; want full NCERT coverage. Product-aware: they compare teachers by name. Evidence: VOC-D05, VOC-D06, VOC-O02.
2. **Board-exam students the night before.** Search a topic name, watch a short animation several times. Problem-aware: "I don't get it", time pressure. Evidence: VOC-T01, VOC-T02, VOC-T03.
3. **Global students in the same chapter** (English-language animation channels, Egypt, Sri Lanka, France). Large but not our core. Evidence: TNKWgcFPHqw and gG7uCskUOrA comment threads.

## Pain Language (ranked by frequency x intensity)
1. "My teacher took hours/weeks and I still didn't get it" (VOC-P02, P03, P04, P05). Table stakes for every animation channel, but it is the reason they click.
2. "Diagrams are static, I can't picture it" (VOC-P06, P07, P08). This is our differentiator: 3D motion of the process itself.
3. Specific confusions: the cardiac cycle graph, AV vs semilunar valves, replication and transcription (VOC-P09 to P12). Each is a short topic.
4. Class 11 loneliness (VOC-P13, P14). Real but not ours to solve; avoid exploiting it.

## Desires & Buying Triggers
- A visual of the process moving (VOC-D01 to D04).
- Proof it matches NCERT (VOC-D05, D06).
- Something they can rewatch before a test (VOC-T01).

## Objections and what dissolves them
- "Short is not enough" (VOC-O02): say it is a revision tool, link the NCERT chapter, pin the one-line summary.
- "Dumbed down" (VOC-O03): keep the exact NCERT terms on screen; the videos already do.

## Watering Holes
YouTube comments under Physics Wallah, Yakeen, Competition Wallah, Prashant Kirad (Hindi-English); Alila Medical Media, Neural Academy, yourgenome, Amoeba Sisters (English animation).

## Dominant Emotion & Sophistication
Frustration turning into relief ("life saver"). Sophisticated market: students already know the "2 minutes beats 1 hour" promise, so the title must name the exact confusion, not the time saved.

## Surprises
- The biggest demand signal is rewatching right before a test, not first learning.
- Students quote the time saved ("2 minutes", "3 minutes") in their own comments; our "50 seconds" fits that language.
- Comments correcting a teacher's mistake get thousands of likes (iUi1M7YkDe4: "you forgot to multiply 2"). Accuracy is noticed and rewarded.
