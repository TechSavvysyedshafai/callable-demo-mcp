    Projectile motion

    NCERT chapter: Motion in a plane. Air resistance is ignored, as in NCERT; g = 9.8 m/s².

    A projectile is anything thrown into the air that then moves only under gravity: a cricket ball, a stone, water from a hose.

    The trick is to split the motion in two. Sideways (horizontal), nothing pushes it, so its horizontal speed never changes. Up and down (vertical), gravity pulls it, so its vertical speed falls by 9.8 m/s every second, reaches zero at the top, then grows downwards.

    Formulas (launch speed u, angle θ)

    Time of flightT = 2u sinθ / g Maximum heightH = u² sin²θ / 2g RangeR = u² sin2θ / g

    Try this

    1. Turn on the velocity arrows and launch. Watch the blue arrow: it stays the same length the whole flight. The red arrow shrinks to nothing at the top, then grows pointing down.

    2. Press “30° and 60°”. Two very different paths land in exactly the same place. Angles that add up to 90° always give the same range, because sin 2θ is the same for both.

    3. Keep the speed fixed and try 40°, 45° and 50°. Which goes furthest?
