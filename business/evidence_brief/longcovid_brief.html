Long COVID: what the research says about death, heart events and time off work

For: life, health, critical illness and disability insurance teams · Date: 6 Nov 2026 · Basis: paper abstracts only (see page 2)
Bottom line. Long COVID is a morbidity story more than a death story. In a large US veterans study, people who were not hospitalised had no extra risk of death after the first year. People who were hospitalised still had about 29% higher death rates in year three. For disability and sick-pay claims the picture is clearer: in a Saudi cohort only 39% of long COVID patients were back at work normally within 12 months, against 82% of those who recovered. About half of people with long COVID at 12 months improved meaningfully by 36 months, and about a quarter recovered fully.

2. How to read the numbers

  • The 4.48 heart-event figure is probably too high. People who get a long COVID diagnosis are often already sicker, and the study compares them with infected people who were not diagnosed. In my judgment (about 70%) a good part of that gap is not caused by long COVID. The 1.53 death figure has the same problem.
  • Different groups, different answers. The veterans cohort is mostly older men in the US. The Saudi and 16-country cohorts are closer to Gulf and South Asian populations but have no uninfected comparison group, so they say less about excess risk.
  • Most studies follow people for up to 3 to 5 years. Nothing here covers 10 years or more.

3. What this could mean for a claims or product team (my judgment, not a finding)

  • Disability and critical illness: the work-return gap is the strongest signal. A claim with a long COVID diagnosis and ongoing symptoms at 12 months is likely to run longer than a typical short-term claim (about 65% in my judgment), but about half of such claimants improve within three years, so it should be reviewed at set intervals, not assumed permanent.
  • Life underwriting: little reason from this evidence to load premiums for people who had COVID and were not hospitalised. A hospitalised case may deserve a look in the first 3 years.
  • Questions to ask: was the person hospitalised, how long have symptoms lasted, any diabetes, reinfections, and a doctor's record of work capacity.
