GLP-1 weight-loss drugs: what the research says about heart risk and death

For: life and health insurance product, underwriting and claims teams · Date: 6 Nov 2026 · Basis: paper abstracts only (see page 2)
Bottom line. In randomised trials of overweight adults without diabetes, GLP-1 and tirzepatide users had about 20% lower odds of heart events and of death from any cause than placebo users. Trials are short and select healthy volunteers, so this does not tell us lifetime mortality for people who stay on the drug, or for those who stop it. Known side effects are mostly digestive. A few rarer ones (thyroid cancer, eye problems, anaesthesia risk) are still being studied.

3. What this could mean for a risk team (my judgment, not a finding)

  • Mortality credit: a case for some credit is plausible (about 55% in my judgment), but the trials follow people for a short time. A credit that assumes lifelong use is not supported by this evidence.
  • Persistence is the key unknown: if many users stop within a year, the benefit may fade. The review above says side effects hurt persistence, but this brief did not find a persistence rate.
  • Claims and underwriting questions worth asking: current or planned surgery or endoscopy, eye history, family or personal thyroid history, how long on the drug and at what dose.
  • Real-world data: a 2025 study emulating a trial in adults with obesity and no diabetes looked at heart and kidney outcomes (Tang 2025). Its results were not read for this brief.
