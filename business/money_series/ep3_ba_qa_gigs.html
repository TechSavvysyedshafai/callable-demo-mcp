Skills, certificates and AI

  • What clients ask for. Insurance domain knowledge (policy, claims, billing), written requirements, test case design, UAT coordination and clear English. Guidewire tool knowledge is often listed. I do not know what you hold, so I assume nothing.
  • ISTQB Foundation (testing). India price about ₹4,700 plus 18% GST, so about ₹5,546 Reported (2026 guide). The US list price is $229 Reported. Sources disagree, so check the invoice.
  • AINS (insurance, The Institutes). About $1,824 to $2,064 for first attempts Reported (secondary guide, not the official price page). It does not expire. Too costly for day 30. Do it later only if clients ask.
  • Not needed to start. No capital means no paid certificate in the first 30 days. Sample work is a cheaper proof (my judgment).
  • AI changes the speed, not the need for judgment. Claude and similar tools can draft test cases, requirement documents and UAT scripts in minutes. You then check them against the real rules. Clients pay for the checking.

  • Use the right purpose code. One source suggests P0802 for software services. Ask your bank which code fits consulting.
  • If the client is an Indian firm, the payment is a domestic invoice, not an export. Different GST rules apply. Ask a CA.

Tax and GST in plain words

Read on 14 Nov 2026 from tax blogs and vendor guides. I could not read the government pages. Please check the GST portal and incometax.gov.in, or ask a CA, before you rely on any line.

  • GST sign-up limit. ₹20 lakh of yearly turnover for service providers (₹10 lakh in a few special category states) Reported. Bihar is not in the special list I saw, but check this. Foreign income counts toward the limit.
  • Overseas clients. Services sold abroad are "zero rated". You charge 0% GST, but only if conditions are met, including payment in foreign money Reported. A Letter of Undertaking (LUT) lets you skip paying GST first. Blogs disagree on whether you can file an LUT before you register.
  • Once registered, returns are still due even when no tax is payable Reported.
  • Income tax, simple scheme. Section 44ADA treats 50% of receipts as profit, with a receipts cap of ₹50 lakh (₹75 lakh if cash receipts are 5% or less) Reported. Whether it suits you depends on your salary and other income. Slabs and rebates were not checked.
  • Salary job. If you have a job, side income adds to it. Keep every invoice and bank record. Read your employment contract for any side-work rule before you start (my judgment).

Scams to avoid

  • Never pay to get a job. Registration fee, ID card, training kit or "refundable security deposit" is the common trap Reported (job-portal blogs). Real employers and real clients do not charge you.
  • Check the sender. A big company offer from a free Gmail or Yahoo address is a warning sign.
  • Keep chat and payment on the platform. Do not move to Telegram or WhatsApp for "faster hiring". Do not do unpaid "test projects" that look like real work.
  • Never share bank PIN, OTP or ID scans before a signed contract on a known platform. Never "receive and forward" money for a client.
  • Report fraud on helpline 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in Reported. I could not read the official advisory text.
  • Expect scam messages. I put that at about 90% within 30 days (my judgment).

30-day plan to a first gig, ₹0 budget

  1. Day 0, rules first. Read your job contract for side-work limits. Never reuse your employer's documents, screens or real customer data. Make every sample from a made-up insurer.
  2. Days 1 to 3, profile. One line: "Insurance BA and UAT tester, policy and claims flows, AI-assisted, human-checked." List only skills you truly have. Do not claim AINS, CPCU, Guidewire or ISTQB unless you hold them. Make a free portfolio page or a shared folder.
  3. Days 4 to 12, three sample deliverables (use Claude to draft, then you fix and own them):
    • Sample 1. A UAT test pack for a made-up motor claim intake flow. About 30 cases, expected results and a traceability sheet.
    • Sample 2. User stories and acceptance rules for a made-up "mid-term policy change" feature. Two pages.
    • Sample 3. A UAT script, a defect log template and a one-page note on how you use AI and check its output.
  4. Days 13 to 25, apply. Send 3 to 5 tailored pitches a day on Upwork, Freelancer.com, Contra and Jobgether, and 10 short notes to Guidewire partners and small offshore QA shops. Use free allowances only. The cost to apply on Upwork was not verified.
  5. Pitch shape, four lines. One: their problem in their words. Two: a link to the closest sample. Three: a small paid first task, such as 20 test cases in 2 days. Four: your price and a question.
  6. Starter price (my estimate). $8 to $12 an hour or a fixed fee per deliverable. Raise it after two good reviews.
  7. Days 26 to 30, review. Count pitches, replies, calls and paid tasks. Improve the sample that got the most clicks.

Kill rule

  • Day 30: fewer than 60 pitches sent means you fixed nothing yet. Fix the effort, not the market.
  • Day 60: 100 or more pitches and no reply or call at all. Stop the marketplace route. Change the samples or the target.
  • Day 90: under ₹10,000 total earned. Stop and take stock. Do not buy courses to "fix" it.
  • Any time: anyone asks you to pay, or you spend more than ₹2,000 of cash, or the work eats more than 12 hours a week. Stop.

Risks and data gaps

  • Experience gap. Insurance BA contracts often ask for 5+ years. Your years are unknown to me.
  • Employer rules. A side-work clause in your job contract could end this plan.
  • Client data. Real policyholder data is private. Do not take it out of a client system. Sign an NDA before seeing any.
  • Price pressure. AI makes drafts cheap. Buyers may expect lower prices (my judgment).
  • Time zone and payment delay. US and UK clients may need overlap hours. Wire payments can take days.
  • Platform risk. Fees can change, and accounts can be suspended.
