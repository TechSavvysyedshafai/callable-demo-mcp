Competition and why price pressure is high

  • Clients can make AI video themselves. HeyGen Creator is about $24 to $29 a month Reported. Runway starts around $12 a month and bills by seconds of footage Reported. Canva Pro in India was listed at ₹500 a month or ₹4,000 a year by one September 2026 blog Reported. Official pages were not read, and these figures conflict across sources.
  • A plain AI video has no moat. If a shop owner can make one for ₹500 a month, you cannot charge much for one. You sell the work around it: the brief, local language, brand fit, fixes, and fast changes (my judgment).
  • Many cheap editors already bid. Reel editors on Freelancer.in bid ₹400 to ₹4,500 a reel Reported.
  • The articles' own tools are open to everyone. That is the point of the promoter pitch, and it is also why a copy-paste studio is not rare (my judgment).

Rules to follow

I am not a lawyer. Most of this comes from law-firm blogs and press. Please check the sources in the notes file.

  • Music: use licensed tracks only. Zee sued Nykaa in the Delhi High Court over 12 reels, claiming about ₹2 crore Reported. The case is not finished. Platform music licences cover personal use. For a business, use Instagram's Sound Collection or a commercially licensed library, and read the licence. The videos in this plan are silent or text-only, which avoids the issue.
  • AI output may not be protected by copyright. The US Copyright Office says purely AI output needs human authorship to be protected, and mixed work is judged case by case Reported. I found nothing on India's rule for AI output. Do not promise a client "you own it exclusively".
  • Do not copy characters or brands. No lookalike cartoon or film characters, no real person faces or voices without written consent (my judgment).
  • Client claims need approval. Prices, offers, "best", "cure", exam "results" and similar claims must come from the client in writing. Keep the message. You are not the one who can prove them (my judgment).
  • Label realistic AI content. India's IT Rules amendment on "synthetically generated information" took effect on 20 Feb 2026. Platforms must label such content clearly Reported. YouTube also asks for disclosure of realistic altered or synthetic content Reported, 2024 coverage. Obviously cartoon or text-on-screen videos are lower risk (my judgment).
  • Clinics and coaching. I did not research the Indian advertising rules for doctors, clinics or coaching classes. Check those before taking such a job. Until then, offer them plain service explainers with no health or result claims.

First-client test (about 2 weeks)

  1. Make 1 sample reel for a made-up shop. Do not use a real brand's logo.
  2. Show it, in person or on WhatsApp, to 10 owners you already know. Ask one question: "I make 6 versions of a 15 s offer reel for ₹1,500. Would you try it for the wedding-season or year-end offer?"
  3. Pass: 2 of 10 say yes to a paid pack, or 1 pays. Anything less means the offer or the sample needs work.
  4. Take 50% upfront by UPI if a client agrees. Send the final file only after the rest is paid (my judgment).

30-day plan, ₹0 budget

  1. Day 0, rules. Read your job contract for side-work limits. Make every sample for a made-up brand. Use silent or text-only videos and no real faces.
  2. Days 1 to 5, template. Ask Claude to build one reusable page with seek(t), one data file, and a render script. Keep it plain HTML. Do not install Remotion yet.
  3. Days 6 to 10, three samples. One shop offer reel, one coaching promo, one product reel. Each 15 s, 3 hooks x 2 CTAs, in Hindi and English.
  4. Days 11 to 25, show them. Show to 20 owners in person or by WhatsApp to people you know. Use the first-client test above. Ask what they would pay.
  5. Days 26 to 30, review. Count owners seen, yes answers, and paid packs. Fix the sample that got most interest.

Kill rule

  • Day 30: fewer than 15 owners shown a sample means you fixed nothing yet. Fix the effort, not the market.
  • Day 60: 40 owners shown and no paid pack. Stop the local route. Change the offer or the target.
  • Day 90: under ₹10,000 total earned. Stop and take stock. Do not buy courses or AI credits to "fix" it.
  • Any time: you spend more than ₹2,000 cash, a client asks for sign-ups you do not understand, or the work eats more than 10 hours a week. Stop.

Risks and data gaps

  • Price pressure. Self-serve AI tools and ₹500 reel editors cap what you can charge.
  • Music and rights. One unlicensed track on a client reel can cause a claim. The Nykaa case shows the stakes.
  • Client claims. A false offer or health claim in your video can come back to you.
  • Employer rules. A side-work clause in your job contract could end this plan.
  • Payment risk. Small clients may pay late. Take part payment upfront.
  • Tool and price changes. Remotion terms, Picsart credits and YouTube thresholds change. The YouTube rise is due on 1 Feb 2027.
  • Scams. Offers to "buy a course" or "pay to join" a creator program are the same trap as in Episode 3.
