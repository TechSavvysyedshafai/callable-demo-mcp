# Idea evaluation (roast, fit, go/no-go, first customers)

Replaces the paid "Roast This Idea", "Check Founder Fit", "Should I Build This" and "Get Customers" agents. The other IdeaBrowser agents (brand, funnel, VSL, emails, landing page) are copywriting tasks: do them on request, no skill needed.

## Rules
1. **Verify before praising.** Check the idea's own claims against the primary source (company post, regulator page, filing). Say what was checked, what was not, and what each source is. Idea-of-the-day scores and "ARR ceilings" are illustrative, not evidence; scores move day to day.
2. **Verified / derived / assumed.** Label every number. Chances are my judgment and say so.
3. **Short and plain.** Answer first, one table, one next step. Percentages, not adjectives.
4. **Fit is about the owner, not the idea.** Use only what the owner has told you: based in India; salaried-job search in progress; insurance BA / UAT / QA background; builds reports and tools with Claude; US cold outreach is possible but slow; little capital; cannot rely on a US presence, licences or a network. If the idea needs US licences, hardware stock, ad-tech contacts, enterprise sales or more than about $2K to test, say so.
5. **Hard stops** (repo rules): no order placement or live trading code; no secrets; no scraping LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram or Reddit; no spam. Contacts come from public pages only, with an opt-out line and a postal address on any commercial email.
6. **Spending needs a yes.** Search, scrape and Apify runs cost credits: say the cost first, cap it, report what was spent. Sending email or posting is never done unprompted.

## Pass 1: Roast (four voices, two lines each)
Customer (would I pay?), competitor (what do I already do?), investor (why will this stay small or large?), mentor (what would I do first?). End with the single most likely reason it fails.

## Pass 2: Founder fit
Score 1 to 5 on: skills, network, capital needed, time to first revenue, regulation or licence risk, location fit. State the two worst gaps. Give one chance: "an owner like this earns $1,000 a month from it within 12 months".

## Pass 3: Go / no-go
Verdict (go, test, or pass), two priced roads (cheap test vs full build), a three-week plan, and a **kill rule written before starting** (for example "fewer than 2 of 5 free tests say they would pay").

## Pass 4: First customers
Where the buyers actually are (public places and communities), which posts the owner must make themselves, a public-source prospect list if allowed, one short message, one follow-up, feedback questions including "what would you pay?", and a tracking sheet.

## Output order
Verdict and one-line reason; what was verified; chances table; fit gaps; plan with kill rule; first customers; next step.
